# Process — Sheet-Pan Roast

A protein and vegetables roasted on trays in the oven. Most of the time is hands-off, and it scales by adding a tray.

## When to pick

- Best for: six or more people, an hour or more, a cook who wants to sit with guests while it cooks.
- Worst for: under forty-five minutes, no oven, a table with one person who does not eat the protein and no second tray to give them.

## Required inputs

- A protein that roasts in under forty minutes, or a second tray of a hearty vegetable for anyone who does not eat it.
- Two or more vegetables that roast at the same temperature.
- Oil, salt, and one strong flavor: garlic, citrus, a spice, or a herb.
- Enough trays that nothing is crowded. Crowded trays steam instead of roast.

## Execution

1. **Set the serving time** and count back fifty-five minutes. Preheat takes fifteen. Roasting takes thirty-five. Resting takes five.

2. **Preheat the oven hot** while cutting everything to the same size, so it all finishes together.

3. **Oil, salt, and season everything** in one bowl so the flavor is even, then spread it on the trays with space between pieces.

4. **Roast, turning once** halfway. Done when the edges are dark and a knife slides into the thickest piece with no resistance.

5. **Rest five minutes** on the trays, then serve from the trays or one big platter.

## Output content

- The shopping list for whatever is missing, including a second tray if one is needed.
- The fifty-five-minute timeline.
- The steps above with the done cue.

## What never to do

- Crowd a tray to save washing one. Use two.
- Mix vegetables that need different temperatures on one tray.
- Skip the rest. It is what keeps the protein juicy.
