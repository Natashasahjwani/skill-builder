# Process — One-Pot Pasta

The pasta cooks in the sauce in one pot, so the starch thickens the sauce and there is one thing to wash. Fastest style this skill knows.

## When to pick

- Best for: under thirty minutes, two to five people, a kitchen with pasta and one good thing to put in it.
- Worst for: a table that needs two different meals, more than five people in a normal-sized pot, a cook who wants to step away from the stove.

## Required inputs

- Pasta, any shape that cooks in under twelve minutes.
- One protein or one main vegetable.
- Something for the sauce: canned tomatoes, cream, stock, or cheese.
- Something sharp to finish: lemon, herbs, or hard cheese.

## Execution

1. **Set the serving time** and count back twenty-five minutes. There is no slack in this style, so start on time.

2. **Brown the protein or vegetable** in the pot first. Take it out.

3. **Add the pasta, the sauce base, and enough liquid to cover.** Bring to a boil, then a steady simmer. Stir every two minutes so nothing sticks.

4. **Cook until the pasta is just tender** and the liquid has thickened to coat it, ten to twelve minutes. If it looks dry before the pasta is done, add a splash of liquid. If it looks soupy, keep simmering with the lid off.

5. **Return the protein, add the sharp finish, taste, and serve** straight from the pot.

## Output content

- The shopping list for whatever of the four inputs is missing.
- The twenty-five-minute timeline.
- The steps above with the cue for the pasta being done.

## What never to do

- Walk away from the pot. It sticks in the time it takes to answer the door.
- Add the sharp finish early. It goes in off the heat.
- Double the batch in a pot that is not big enough. Make two pots.
