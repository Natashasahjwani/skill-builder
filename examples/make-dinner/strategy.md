# Make Dinner — Strategy

This document picks which meal style to run tonight. It decides. It does not cook. The cooking is in `processes/`.

## What to load before deciding

- The constraints from step one of `SKILL.md`: people, diets, minutes, and what is in the kitchen.
- `processes/INDEX.md`, for the list of styles.

## First decision: how much time

- Under thirty minutes: only styles with no oven preheat and one pot or pan. That leaves one-pot-pasta.
- Thirty to sixty minutes: any style.
- Over sixty minutes: any style, and lean toward the one the cook enjoys most.

## Second decision: who is eating

- Anyone vegetarian or with a strong dislike of one main ingredient: styles where each person builds their own plate cover this without a second dish. That points to taco-bar.
- Everyone eats the same things: any style.
- Six or more people: styles that scale by adding a tray or a bigger pot, not by doubling the hands-on work. That points to sheet-pan-roast or taco-bar.

## Third decision: what is on hand

- Pasta, one protein or one vegetable, and something creamy or acidic: one-pot-pasta.
- Tortillas or something to wrap in, plus two or three toppings: taco-bar.
- A protein and two vegetables that can share an oven: sheet-pan-roast.
- Nothing that fits: the style with the shortest shopping list wins.

## Which process

Take the styles that survived all three decisions. If one remains, run it. If two or more remain, run the one that needs the least shopping. If none remain, relax the time decision first, then the on-hand decision, and re-run. Never relax the diet decision.
